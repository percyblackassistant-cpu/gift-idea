Third on the list

A bag that folds into itself.

A packable backpack is the spare bag that lives in a suitcase: nothing much on its own, then a full daypack at the other end of the trip. The trick is buying one that still behaves like a bag when it is open. This Patagonia does, at 370 grams and 27 litres.

Why it works

Three reasons this is the pick when the brief says good quality rather than cheapest.

The fabric is the quality

70-denier recycled nylon ripstop, with a silicone face coating and a PU back coating. Thin enough to stuff into a pocket, woven so a small tear stops instead of running.

It carries two ways

Shoulder straps that tuck away, so it works as a tote for a market run and a backpack for a day out. Stretch bottle pockets on both sides, which most packables skip.

Pockets where they count

A main zip that opens wide, an internal zip pocket with a key keeper, and an outside organiser pocket for the phone and the map. Nothing fussy, nothing missing.

!

What packability costs: structure. There is no frame and the straps are thin, so 27 litres of soft bag slumps once it is half full. Perfect for a jacket, water and a market shop. Not the bag to carry a laptop and a full commute every day.

If you are buying it

The name to ask for, the price band, and the one check that matters.

  • 1 Ask for the Patagonia Ultralight Black Hole Tote Pack, 27 litres. It is a tote with tuck-away backpack straps, not a framed rucksack.
  • 2 Expect £76 to £85. Above £85 is over RRP, and under £70 is a good day worth taking.
  • 3 Check it stuffs into its own pocket before buying. That is the whole feature, and a packable that will not pack is just a floppy bag.
  • 4 If it is only ever a spare bag for flights, the Osprey at £45 does the job lighter and cheaper. The Patagonia buys better pockets and a nicer carry for the extra money.
